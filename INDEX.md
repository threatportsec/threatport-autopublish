## Why this order

Two sets were written, and they behave differently.

**The feed set** was written for someone who does not know the problem exists.
Strong hooks, spikes on Instagram, then fades.

**The search set** was written against phrasing pulled from Google and Bing
autocomplete, so the titles match what people actually type. On YouTube these
keep earning views for months — every day one is not published is a day of
accrual lost, which is why they are not saved for the end.

The order mixes them in a repeating ten-day shape so YouTube builds a search
library from day one while Instagram still gets the punchy ones.

## Before day one

**The first hour matters more than the video.** Replying to comments in the
first hour does more for reach than anything in the file.

**Do not change the link.** Every description and bio points at https://threatport.com/#hero-scan —
consistency is what makes the traffic attributable.

**Set `cover.jpg` manually** on both platforms. The automatic frame grab crops
badly; the cover is built for the 4:5 grid.

**Review at day 10.** By then there is real signal on which topics land — better
than any keyword tool, and it should decide what the next set is about.

**Days with practitioner videos will underperform on Instagram.** That is
expected. They are there for YouTube search and for the people who recommend
tools. Judge them on search impressions, not Reels views.

`youtube-metadata.csv` carries the same titles and descriptions in one file for
bulk upload tools.
